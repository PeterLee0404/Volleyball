# Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC) Regimes & Limb Stiffness Dynamics

The stretch-shortening cycle (SSC) represents an eccentric pre-stretch immediately preceding a concentric contraction, exploiting stored elastic strain energy and stretch-reflex potentiation.

## 1. Data-Driven SSC Tripartite Classification

| Regime | Ground Contact Time (GCT) | Primary Volleyball Movement | Biomechanical Mechanism | Primary Limiting Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Short-SSC** | $< 188\text{ ms}$ | Immediate repeated block jumps, reactive bounds | Short-latency stretch reflex, high tendon stiffness, minimal joint flexion | Tendon stiffness, neuromuscular pre-activation |
| **Mid-SSC** | $188\text{ ms} \le \text{GCT} < 222\text{ ms}$ | Standard rebound jumps | Moderate coupling time | Reactive Strength Index (RSI) |
| **Long-SSC** | $\ge 222\text{ ms}$ | Transition spike jumps, approach attacks, depth jumps | Large joint excursion, active muscular work, propulsive impulse | Peak propulsion velocity, ankle moment, knee extension ROM |

## 2. Long-SSC Biomechanics: Propulsion Over Braking

In female volleyball players executing 40 cm depth jumps ($\text{GCT} = 472 \pm 89\text{ ms}$, clearly Long-SSC):
- **Primary Predictor of Jump Height**: **Peak propulsion velocity** ($r = 0.911, p < 0.001$).
- **Joint Kinetic Contribution**: Ankle moment ($r = 0.567, p < 0.05$) dominates over knee ($r = 0.008$) and hip moments ($r = 0.114$).
- **Braking vs Propulsion**: Peak braking force ($r = -0.073$) and braking power ($r = -0.021$) do not correlate with jump height. Peak propulsive impulse ($r = 0.500, p < 0.001$) and propulsion power ($r = 0.521, p < 0.05$) determine the outcome.

## 3. The Leg Stiffness Paradox
In high-performing jumpers (HJG vs LJG):
- High jumpers exhibited **significantly LOWER vertical leg stiffness** ($58.44 \text{ vs } 84.66\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}, p = 0.004, g = -1.521$).
- High jumpers exhibited **significantly LOWER peak braking force** ($17.00 \text{ vs } 21.78\text{ N}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}, p = 0.013$).
- **Mechanism**: Long-SSC tasks require knee and hip compliance to lower the center of mass, lengthening the acceleration path and contact duration to produce maximum positive impulse. Excessive stiffness limits countermovement depth and forces an inefficient, jarring landing.
