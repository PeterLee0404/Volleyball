# Effects of lower limb biomechanical characteristics on jump performance in female volleyball players based on long Stretch–Shortening cycle movements

- **Authors**: Beiwang Deng, Yueming Li, Gesheng Lin, Ruixiang Yan, Jianxin He, Duanying Li, Jian Sun (2025)
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology (doi: 10.3389/fbioe.2025.1653751)
- **Cohort**: 18 trained female collegiate volleyball players (Age 19.17 ± 0.79 yr, Height 172.56 ± 5.40 cm, 5.69 ± 2.44 yr training; split into High Jump Group HJG n=9 and Low Jump Group LJG n=9).
- **Task**: 40 cm Depth Jump (Long-SSC: Ground Contact Time ≥ 222 ms).
- **Core Findings**:
  - Peak propulsion velocity had strongest correlation with jump height (r=0.911, p<0.001).
  - Propulsion-phase metrics (power, impulse, ankle moment) dominated over braking-phase metrics.
  - Ankle joint moment during propulsion was significantly greater in HJG (-1.492 vs -1.686 N·m·kg⁻¹, p=0.010, g=1.316) and correlated strongly with height (r=0.567).
  - Paradoxical lower limb stiffness: HJG had significantly LOWER leg stiffness (58.44 vs 84.66 N·m⁻¹·kg⁻¹, p=0.004, g=-1.521) and LOWER peak braking force (17.00 vs 21.78 N·kg⁻¹, p=0.013, g=-1.257).
  - Mechanism: Long-SSC movements allow a deeper countermovement and longer contact time, converting eccentric displacement into large propulsive work rather than relying on short-range tendon recoil.
