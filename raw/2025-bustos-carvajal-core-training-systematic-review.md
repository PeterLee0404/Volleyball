# Exploring the role of the core in sports performance: a systematic review of the effects of core muscle training

- **Authors**: Juan Sebastian Bustos Carvajal, Florencio Arias Coronel (2025)
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Sports and Active Living (doi: 10.3389/fspor.2025.1630584)
- **Design**: Systematic Review of 7 controlled clinical trials (n=8–30 per study; sports: soccer, volleyball, basketball, badminton, running, pencak silat).
- **Core Findings**:
  - Consistent positive effects: Trunk extensor maximal isometric force (MIF) (+3.8% to +6.8%, p=0.02), core endurance (planks, McGill battery: FET, EET, LET, p<0.01), dynamic balance (SEBT across anterior, posteromedial, posterolateral, p<0.05), and short sprint speed (10–20 m split, p<0.001).
  - Inconclusive / No significant transfer: Countermovement jump (CMJ) height (e.g., Prieske 2016, Tsai 2020: p=0.63–0.82), agility tests (T-Test, Illinois Agility: p=0.07–0.83), trunk flexor MIF (p=0.11–0.47).
  - Conclusion: Isolated core training provides baseline postural stability and power transmission efficiency, but does not substitute for sport-specific multi-joint power training.
