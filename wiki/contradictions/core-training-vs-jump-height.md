# The Core Strength Paradox: Postural Stability vs Jump Performance Transfer

## The Conflict
Athletic conditioning culture heavily promotes core training (planks, stability balls, bird-dogs) as a universal power amplifier. However, systematic review evidence indicates that **dedicated core training programs show NO statistically significant transfer to vertical jump height (CMJ) or change-of-direction agility** ($p > 0.05$).

## Context Variable Breakdown

| Athlete Metric | Transfer Level | Statistical Evidence | Explanatory Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trunk Extensor Strength** | **High** | $+3.8\% \text{ to } +6.8\%, p = 0.02$ | Direct neuromuscular overload of erector spinae / multifidus. |
| **10–20 m Sprint Speed** | **High** | $-2.7\% \text{ to } -3.6\%, p < 0.001$ | Prevents pelvic tilt/wobble during high horizontal propulsive shear forces. |
| **Dynamic Balance (SEBT)** | **High** | Large effect size, $p < 0.05$ | Enhanced proprioceptive and anticipatory postural adjustments (APAs). |
| **Vertical Jump (CMJ)** | **Zero / Inconclusive** | $p = 0.63 - 0.82$ | Vertical jump height is governed by rate of force development (RFD) in hip, knee, and ankle extensors, not isometric trunk endurance. |
| **Agility (T-Test, Illinois)** | **Zero / Inconclusive** | $p = 0.07 - 0.83$ | Agility requires reactive perceptual-cognitive decision-making and rapid eccentric deceleration in the lower limbs. |

## The Resolution: Foundational vs Driver
- **The Core as a Conduit, Not an Engine**: The core does not generate vertical propulsion; it simply prevents energy leaks across the spine.
- **When Core Training Improves Jumps**: Only in athletes with acute trunk instability where spinal collapse dampens leg drive. In trained athletes, the limiting factor is triple extension power (Vastus Lateralis, Gastrocnemius, Gluteus), not trunk stiffness.
- **Rule of Thumb**: Core endurance and anti-rotation work are mandatory for injury resilience (knee valgus control on landings) and acceleration posture, but will **never substitute** for heavy squats, Olympic lifting, or plyometrics to raise vertical jump height.
