# Caffeine (1,3,7-Trimethylxanthine)

Caffeine is a potent central nervous system (CNS) stimulant that directly modulates neuromuscular recruitment and delays central fatigue during high-intensity athletic tasks.

## 1. Physiological Mechanism
- **Adenosine Receptor Antagonism**: Competitively binds to $A_1$ and $A_{2A}$ adenosine receptors in the central nervous system, preventing adenosine-mediated central inhibition.
- **Enhanced Descending Motor Drive**: Increases corticospinal excitability, augmenting motor unit firing rates and motor unit synchronization during maximal ballistic efforts (jumps, sprints).
- **Peripheral Excitation-Contraction Coupling**: Increases sarcoplasmic reticulum $Ca^{2+}$ release and sensitivity of the myofilaments.
- **RPE Attenuation**: Substantially lowers rating of perceived exertion (sRPE) under high metabolic or mental fatigue.

## 2. Dosing Protocol & Timing
- **Effective Acute Dosage**: $3 - 6\text{ mg/kg}$ body mass.
  - *Example (80 kg Athlete)*: $240 - 480\text{ mg}$.
- **Timing**: Ingested 45–60 minutes prior to competition or high-intensity jump training.
- **Tolerance & Habituation**: Habitual high caffeine users ($> 300\text{ mg/day}$) show reduced acute ergogenic magnitude; a 4–7 day pre-competition taper restores receptor sensitivity.

## 3. High-Intensity & Vertical Jump Application
- Directly enhances jump height ($+2 - 4\%$ CMJ gain) and sustains peak propulsion velocity across repeated jump bouts by countering group III/IV afferent central inhibition.
