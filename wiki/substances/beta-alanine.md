# Beta-Alanine

Beta-alanine is the rate-limiting precursor in the synthesis of the intramuscular dipeptide **carnosine** ($\beta\text{-alanyl-L-histidine}$), the primary physicochemical intracellular buffer in Type II fast-twitch muscle fibers.

## 1. Physiological Mechanism
- **Proton ($H^+$) Neutralization**: High-intensity glycolytic exercise yields rapid proton accumulation, dropping intramuscular pH from ~7.1 to $< 6.5$, inhibiting phosphofructokinase (PFK) and disrupting cross-bridge attachment.
- **Carnosine Buffer**: Elevated carnosine absorbs excess protons, delaying metabolic acidosis and extending the duration of high-intensity efforts lasting between **30 seconds and 10 minutes**.

## 2. Dosing Protocol
- **Daily Saturation Dose**: $3.2 - 6.4\text{ g/day}$, divided into smaller doses of $0.8 - 1.6\text{ g}$ every 3–4 hours (prevents acute paresthesia / tingling sensation).
- **Loading Period**: Requires 4–6 weeks of continuous daily supplementation to elevate intramuscular carnosine stores by 40–80%.
- **Maintenance**: $1.2\text{ g/day}$ sustains elevated levels.

## 3. Sports Application
- Essential for team-sport repeated high-intensity efforts (e.g. repeated spike jumps, long volleyball rally transitions, repeated sprint ability RSA).
