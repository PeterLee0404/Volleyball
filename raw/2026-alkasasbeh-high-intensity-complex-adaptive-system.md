# High-Intensity Exercise Performance as a Complex Adaptive System: An Integrative Review

- **Authors**: Walaa Jumah Alkasasbeh, Adam Tawfiq Amawi, Gerasimos V. Grivas (2026)
- **Journal**: Muscles (doi: 10.3390/muscles5030053)
- **Framework**: Complex Adaptive Systems (CAS) & Network Physiology applied to athletic performance.
- **Core Insights**:
  - Non-linear & emergent performance: Performance is not the sum of isolated parameters (e.g. VO2max + 1RM), but an emergent property of interacting neuromuscular, bioenergetic, biomechanical, and molecular subsystems.
  - Molecular decision nodes: Reciprocal crosstalk between AMPK (energy-sensing, mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α) and mTOR (mechanosensitive hypertrophy via FAK/integrins and YAP/TAZ).
  - Redox Signaling: ROS acts as an essential trigger for PGC-1α, AMPK, and NRF2. Excessive antioxidant supplementation blunts training adaptations.
  - Neuromuscular fatigue: Multilevel feedback loops (central motor cortex drive -> spinal α-motoneurons -> group III/IV afferents sensing H+ and Pi -> feedback inhibition).
  - Ergogenic aids as system modulators:
    - Caffeine: Adenosine receptor antagonism, central drive, motor unit recruitment under fatigue.
    - Creatine: Immediate PCr resynthesis, cellular hydration, anabolic signaling.
    - Beta-Alanine: Intracellular carnosine elevation, H+ buffering for high-intensity efforts (30s to several minutes).
    - Nitrates: Nitric oxide bioavailability, oxygen economy, reduction of ATP oxygen cost.
