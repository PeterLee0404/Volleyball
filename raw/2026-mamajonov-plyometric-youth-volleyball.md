# Effects of Plyometric Training on Jump Biomechanics, Explosive Strength, and Jump Endurance in Adolescent Volleyball Players Aged 13–14 Years

- **Authors**: Dilshodbek Mamajonov, Nazokat Tukhtaboeva, Alisher Izatulayev (2026)
- **Journal**: Physiologia (doi: 10.3390/physiologia6020034)
- **Cohort**: 30 male volleyball players aged 13–14 years (EG n=15, CG n=15).
- **Intervention**: 8-week progressive plyometric training (3 sessions/week, 75–85 min/session with 35–45 min plyo, 3:1 loading cycle).
  - Weeks 1–2 (Adaptation): Forefoot jumps, lateral line jumps, skipping. 60–80 contacts/session, 180–240/week. 60–75% effort. Rest 30–60s.
  - Weeks 3–4 (Dev I): Squat jumps, tuck jumps, box jumps, hurdle jumps. 80–100 contacts/session, 240–300/week. 70–85% effort. Rest 45–90s.
  - Weeks 5–6 (Dev II): Repeated vertical, unilateral, zig-zag, approach jumps. 90–110 contacts/session, 270–330/week. 80–90% effort. Rest 60–120s.
  - Weeks 7–8 (Sport-specific): Block jumps, spike jumps, drop jumps (20–40cm), lateral-to-vertical combinations. 100–120 contacts/session, 300–360/week. 85–95% effort. Rest 90–120s.
- **Outcomes**: Significant Group × Time interaction across all RCMJ variables (CMJ height +3.88cm vs +2.04cm, concentric power +5.82kW, peak speed +0.29m/s, Endurance Index +0.15) and field tests (Standing Long Jump +30.47cm, Running 2-leg Jump +5.73cm, Abalakov +5.47cm, reduced error in spike endurance).
