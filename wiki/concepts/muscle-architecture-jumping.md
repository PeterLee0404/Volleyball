# Muscle Architecture Determinants of Vertical Jump

Lower extremity muscle architecture—specifically anatomical cross-sectional area (ACSA) and fascicle length (FL)—governs force output and maximal contraction velocity in jumping athletes.

## 1. Key Muscle Predictors

| Muscle Group | Architectural Parameter | Jump Type Specificity | Explanatory Variance | Physiological Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vastus Lateralis (VL)** | **ACSA** (Cross-Sectional Area) | Universal (AJ, CMJ, SJ, DJ) | $R^2 = 0.25 - 0.52$ | Primary driver of knee extension torque during explosive extension. |
| **Lateral Gastrocnemius (LG)** | **FL** (Fascicle Length) | Specific to **Attack Jump (AJ)** | $R^2 = 0.18$ ($p=0.009$) | Permits higher shortening velocities ($V_{\max}$) in the distal kinetic chain. |
| **Rectus Femoris (RF)** | All (ACSA, MT, PA, FL) | **No correlation** ($p > 0.05$) | $R^2 \approx 0$ | Multi-joint length stabilization (see paradox below). |

## 2. The Rectus Femoris (RF) Paradox
Despite contributing ~24% to knee extension torque in isolated testing, **RF architecture does not predict vertical jump height**:
- **Bi-articular Constraint**: During the concentric jumping phase, the knee extends while the hip simultaneously extends. 
- **Isometric Action**: Because the knee and hip torques and displacement velocities are nearly matched, the RF maintains an almost quasi-isometric length, performing minimal positive mechanical work on the skeleton.
- **Implication**: Training isolated knee extension on a leg extension machine to hypertrophy the rectus femoris does not translate well into vertical jumping gains.

## 3. Attack Jump (AJ) Predictive Model
In elite male volleyball athletes, combining knee extensor hypertrophy, calf fascicle length, and training age predicts **73% of jump height variance**:

$$\text{AJ Height} = f(\text{VL-ACSA}, \text{LG-FL}, \text{Training Age}) \quad (F = 13.86, R^2 = 0.73, p < 0.001)$$

- **Standardized $\beta$**: VL-ACSA ($\beta = 0.65$), LG-FL ($\beta = 0.46$), Training Age ($\beta = -0.35$).
- **Fascicle Length Mechanism**: Longer muscle fascicles contain more sarcomeres in series. According to Hill's force-velocity relationship, this allows higher shortening velocities under submaximal load, translating to greater push-off velocity at takeoff.
